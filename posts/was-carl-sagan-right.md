## What did Sagan predict?

In 1995, a year before his death, astronomer Carl Sagan published *The Demon-Haunted World: Science as a Candle in the Dark*. Near the beginning of the book, he sketched a dark vision of the country his descendants would inherit:

> *"I have a foreboding of an America in my children's or grandchildren's time — when the United States is a service and information economy; when nearly all the manufacturing industries have slipped away to other countries; when awesome technological powers are in the hands of a very few, and no one representing the public interest can even grasp the issues; when the people have lost the ability to set their own agendas or knowledgeably question those in authority; when, clutching our crystals and nervously consulting our horoscopes, our critical faculties in decline, unable to distinguish between what feels good and what's true, we slide, almost without noticing, back into superstition and darkness...*

> *The dumbing down of America is most evident in the slow decay of substantive content in the enormously influential media, the 30 second sound bites (now down to 10 seconds or less), lowest common denominator programming, credulous presentations on pseudoscience and superstition, but especially a kind of celebration of ignorance."*

Three decades later, Sagan's grandchildren are adults, and the passage [goes viral every few months](https://www.reddit.com/r/Damnthatsinteresting/comments/x6jq5w/carl_sagan_predicted_the_decline_of_our_time_27/), shared by millions of people scrolling ten-second clips on feeds built by a handful of trillion-dollar companies. The medium has become part of the evidence.

But recognition is not proof. Sagan's passage is a set of testable claims about jobs, power, media, belief and cognition, and thirty years of data can now grade them.

**The short answer:** Sagan was right about the factories and the concentration of power, right but early about the media, and mostly right about superstition, which now travels through feeds rather than horoscopes. On his central charge, declining critical faculties, he was wrong for seventeen years after he wrote and has been increasingly right since 2012. And the decline is not uniform. It is an hourglass: the most skilled Americans are holding or gaining, the least skilled are falling fast, and the middle is thinning. A rich, technically fluent few above a swelling base that cannot judge what it reads is not a detour from Sagan's nightmare. It is how it arrives.

## How did his predictions fare?

| Sagan's prediction | What the data show | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Factories move abroad; a service economy | Manufacturing fell from 21.7% of jobs in 1979 to 8.0% in 2026; services hold 86.4% | Came true |
| Technological power in a very few hands | Industry built 91% of notable AI models in 2025; Musk alone has put more than $650 million into the last two federal elections | Came true |
| Representatives cannot grasp the issues | Congress closed its technology office in 1995 and never fully rebuilt it | Mostly true |
| Shrinking sound bites, shallower media | 42 seconds in 1968, under 10 by 1988; news is now moving to feeds | Came true |
| A slide back into superstition | Astrology belief flat since 1990, but health and political myths spread through feeds, and measles is at a 35-year high | Mostly true |
| Critical faculties in decline | Gains until about 2012, then an hourglass: the top holds or gains, the bottom falls, the middle thins | Partly true |

## 1. Did the factories leave and the tech barons take over?

Yes, and the second half matters more. Manufacturing held 21.7% of U.S. payroll jobs in 1979, 14.7% when Sagan wrote and [8.0% in September 2026](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MANEMP): 12.65 million of 159 million jobs. [Service industries](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SRVPRD) now hold 86.4%.

![Since Sagan wrote, factory jobs fell 27% while factory output rose 43%](sagan-manufacturing-jobs-vs-output.png)

Rising output complicates the story without overturning it. Nearly all of the gain came before 2007, and much of it reflects [quality adjustments for faster computer chips](https://www.upjohn.org/research-highlights/understanding-decline-manufacturing-employment). Trade did more damage than robots: [Chinese import competition alone cost an estimated 2.0 to 2.4 million U.S. jobs](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20395) from 1999 to 2011.

The "very few" can be counted. Industry built [93 of 102 notable AI models in 2025](https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026_chapter_1_research_development.pdf); academia built two. The ten largest companies held [almost 40% of the S&P 500](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/research/article/in-the-shadows-of-giants/) in mid-2025, the highest share since the mid-1960s, and four of them plan [about $725 billion of capital spending](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/article/meta-microsoft-amazon-and-alphabet-are-about-to-spend-a-shocking-amount-of-money-to-dominate-the-ai-era-115359575.html) in 2026, mostly on AI.

What turned tech giants into tech barons was a court ruling, not a product. [Citizens United](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/how-citizens-united-reshaped-elections) let corporations and the very rich spend without limit through super PACs from 2010. Those groups spent about $6.4 billion on federal races through 2022 and a record $2.7 billion in 2024 alone, and billionaires supplied 15% of all federal election money in the 2022 midterms.

Elon Musk shows what that money buys. He spent [more than $290 million](https://ktvz.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2025/02/01/elon-musk-spent-more-than-290-million-on-the-2024-election-year-end-fec-filings-show/) to elect Donald Trump and other Republicans in 2024, then ran the administration's DOGE cost-cutting drive while Tesla and SpaceX held at least $38 billion in federal contracts. The two fell out publicly in 2025 and have since reconciled: on October 8 Trump gave Musk the National Medal of Science, as Musk was pouring [at least $366 million into the 2026 midterms](https://www.forbes.com/sites/alisondurkee/2026/10/12/musk-bankrolls-republicans-in-midterms-as-biggest-donor-while-attacking-talarico/), more than any other donor. It looks less like lobbying, more like friends with benefits.

The rest of the industry paid at the door. Meta, Amazon and OpenAI's Sam Altman [gave $1 million each](https://www.nbcconnecticut.com/news/business/money-report/tech-companies-most-threatened-by-trump-are-donating-to-his-inauguration-fund/3454577/) to Trump's inaugural fund, as did [Microsoft](https://gulfnews.com/world/americas/microsoft-google-adobe-give-1-million-each-to-donald-trump-inaugural-fund-1.500013613). Apple, Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Palantir and Coinbase are among the [donors to the new White House ballroom](https://www.fortune.com/2025/10/26/37-white-house-ballroom-donors-funding-300-million-build-tech-ceos-trump). An AI-industry super PAC, [Leading the Future](https://news.bgov.com/private-equity/andreessen-horowitz-fuel-ai-industrys-51-million-midterm-haul), raised $51 million for the midterms, half of it from Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz.

## 2. Did the media decay into sound bites?

Yes, but by 1995 that was already history. Harvard's Kiku Adatto [measured candidate sound bites](https://shorensteincenter.org/resource/sound-bite-democracy-campaign-coverage-1968-and-1988/) on network evening news at 42.3 seconds in 1968 and 9.8 in 1988, and they have [hovered near 8 seconds](https://www.academia.edu/21845529/Taking_Television_Seriously_A_Sound_and_Image_Bite_Analysis_of_Presidential_Campaign_Coverage_1992_2004) since.

![The sound bite stopped shrinking by 1988; the news has since moved to the feed](sagan-sound-bites-and-tiktok-news.png)

Two things changed instead. Television kept the picture and dropped the argument: "image bites," candidates seen but not heard, grew from 22 to 26 seconds per story between 1992 and 2004 and outlasted the sound bites. Then the news moved to the feed: [one in five U.S. adults](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/10/19/1-in-5-americans-regularly-get-news-on-tiktok/) now regularly gets news on TikTok, as do nearly four in ten under 30.

## 3. Are we sliding back into superstition?

Mostly yes, though not the way Sagan pictured it. Old-fashioned superstition is flat: in [Pew's 2024 survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/), 27% of adults said they believe in astrology, statistically unchanged from 2017, and [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/16915/Three-Four-Americans-Believe-Paranormal.aspx) found about the same in 1990.

![Belief in astrology has been flat for 35 years](sagan-astrology-belief.png)

The new superstition arrives through the feed. A quarter of adults use social media for health information at least weekly, and they are more than twice as likely to believe the MMR vaccine causes autism: [37%, against 16%](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-tracking-poll-on-health-information-and-trust-update-on-common-vaccine-myths/) of those who never do. Few Americans are ardent believers in any one myth, but 31% sit in a "mixed middle," unsure what is true. Political news is sorted the same way: among [208 million U.S. Facebook users](https://csmapnyu.org/research/academic-research/asymmetric-ideological-segregation-in-exposure-to-political-news-on-facebook) in 2020, exposure was highly segregated by ideology, and more so in what people engaged with than in what they could have seen.

Trust in science was dented, not broken: confidence that scientists act in the public interest [fell from 87% in April 2020 to 73% in 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2026/01/15/americans-confidence-in-scientists/), then recovered to 77%.

Where doubt meets biology, it becomes disease. A record 4.2% of kindergartners had a vaccine exemption in 2025–26, and two-dose MMR coverage [slid from 95.2% to 92.4%](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13322.html), below the roughly 95% needed for herd immunity. The country's measles elimination status, held since 2000, is now in jeopardy.

![Measles is back: 2026 already has the most U.S. cases since 1991](sagan-measles-cases.png)

The skill that would prevent this is the one Sagan cared about most, and the OECD estimates that [more than half of 15-year-olds](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2025-results-volume-i_73451bc5-en) may be vulnerable to misinformation. **Horoscopes are flat; superstition has moved into the feed, and the inability to tell a reliable claim from a viral one is the real candle going out.**

## 4. Did IQ scores rise, and are they now falling?

They rose for most of a century. [James Flynn found](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.95.1.29) that U.S. IQ scores climbed about 3 points per decade from 1932 to 1978, and a [meta-analysis of 285 studies](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037173) confirmed the gain. Better nutrition, longer schooling and work that rewarded abstract thought did the teaching. What an environment can teach, it can untrain.

![Where IQ scores reversed, they fell about as fast as they once rose](sagan-iq-rise-and-reversal.png)

The strongest evidence of a reversal is Norwegian. Among [736,808 conscripts born 1962–1991](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1718793115), scores peaked with the 1975 birth cohort and then fell about a third of a point a year. Because the decline shows up between brothers, it cannot be a change in who has children; it is environmental. [Danish scores](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0261117) turned down after the 1980 cohort.

The American evidence is thinner. Among [394,378 adults who took a free online test](https://personality-project.org/revelle/publications/dworak.23.pdf) from 2006 to 2018, scores fell most among 18- to 22-year-olds. The sample is self-selected, so treat it as a warning light, not a verdict: the reversal is proven in Scandinavia and plausible in the U.S.

## 5. Are American students learning less?

Yes, and the losses run from the bottom up. NAEP's long-term trend has tested 13-year-olds with largely the same questions since the early 1970s. Four decades of gains ended in 2012; by 2025, math was back to its 1990 level and reading to its [1971 level](https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/ltt/2025/supporting-files/2025_technical_and_results_appendix_ltt.pdf).

![Thirteen-year-olds peaked in 2012 and were slipping before schools closed](sagan-naep-age-13-trend.png)

The shape is an hourglass. Since 2013, [the weakest eighth-graders](https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/DataService/GetAdhocData.aspx?type=data&subject=mathematics&grade=8&subscale=MRPCM&variable=TOTAL&jurisdiction=NT&stattype=PC:P1,PC:P9,MN:MN&Year=2013,2019,2022,2024) have lost about six times as much ground as the strongest; in reading, half of that loss came before 2020. In 2024, eighth-grade math was flat overall only because [higher performers gained while lower performers fell](https://www.nagb.gov/powered-by-naep/the-2024-nations-report-card/10-takeaways-from-2024-naep-results), and twelfth-graders posted the [lowest scores ever reported](https://www.nagb.gov/powered-by-naep/the-2024-nations-report-card/top-5-takeaways-from-12-grade-naep-math-reading-results.html), with 45% below Basic in math.

![Since 2013 the weakest eighth-graders lost six times as much as the strongest](sagan-naep-grade-8-percentiles.png)

Internationally, the U.S. is falling more slowly than its peers. [PISA 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2025-results-volume-i_73451bc5-en/full-report/student-performance-in-pisa-2025_23e075c2.html), released September 8, 2026, put U.S. 15-year-olds at record lows in math and reading. The OECD average fell further, so U.S. rankings [rose to 13th in reading and 27th in math](https://k12dive.com/news/from-science-to-screen-time-4-key-takeaways-from-the-latest-pisa-results/829867).

![U.S. math and reading hit record lows, but the OECD average fell faster](sagan-pisa-us-vs-oecd.png)

Two cautions apply. Over 40% of U.S. students [skipped the background questionnaire](https://chalkbeat.org/2026/09/08/pisa-results-2025-show-us-gaining-as-other-countries-decline) after a testing pause in spring 2025, so the OECD could not rule out bias. And by the OECD's own estimate that U.S. students gain 12 to 14 points a school year, the 14-point reading drop is roughly a year of learning.

## 6. Is it just the kids?

No. Adults are slipping too, so schools cannot be the whole story. On the OECD's Survey of Adult Skills, U.S. literacy held steady from 2012 to 2017, then [fell 13 points by 2023](https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/piaac/2023/national_results.asp); numeracy fell 6. The share of adults at or below Level 1 in literacy, able to handle only short, simple texts, rose from 19% to 28%.

![Adult literacy fell most where it was already lowest](sagan-adult-literacy-by-education.png)

Here too the shape is an hourglass. The NCES commissioner described a ["dwindling middle"](https://ies.ed.gov/learn/press-release/u-s-adults-score-par-international-average-literacy-skills-below-international-average-numeracy-and): more adults clustered at the bottom, a rising share at the very top of numeracy, and a gap between the most and least skilled that is the widest of any participating country. Across the OECD, [only Finland and Denmark improved](https://educacionfpydeportes.gob.es/dam/jcr:a8e49b84-bc36-4225-ba3e-353fa97c7f6b/insightsinterpretations.pdf) in literacy. The 2023 test changed format, so the size of the drop deserves care; its direction does not.

The habit that builds these skills is fading at every age, [fastest among lower-income, less-educated and rural Americans](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2025/aug/proportion-americans-reading-pleasure-fell-40-over-20-years).

![Reading for pleasure is down on every long-run measure](sagan-reading-for-pleasure.png)

## 7. What is driving the decline?

No single cause fits all the evidence. Any explanation has to account for a turn around 2012, before the pandemic; losses concentrated among the weakest; and similar declines among adults and across rich countries.

| Explanation | Evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smartphones and social media | Teen smartphone access rose from [37% in 2012](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2013/03/13/teens-and-technology-2013/) to [95% in 2018](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2018/05/31/teens-social-media-technology-2018/), matching the turn. A strict [Florida phone ban](https://nber.org/digest/202512/school-cell-phone-bans-and-student-achievement) raised scores about 1 percentile, and [causal evidence of harm is contested](https://developingadolescent.semel.ucla.edu/news/item/the-great-rewiring-is-social-media-really-behind-an-epidemic-of-teenage-mental-illness) | Plausible contributor; measured effects modest |
| Less reading, more switching | Daily reading for pleasure fell from 28% to 16% of Americans; screen-switching time fell to about 47 seconds | Likely mechanism |
| Retreat from test-based accountability after 2013 | Scores [rose fastest during the accountability era](https://wjla.com/news/crisis-in-the-classroom/story/reading-scores-have-dropped-for-10-years-a-dartmouth-professor-explains-why) | Part of the U.S. school story; cannot explain adults or other countries |
| The pandemic and chronic absence | Chronic absenteeism [rose from about 15% to 28.3%](https://www.aei.org/research-products/report/from-attendance-crisis-to-chronic-condition-tracking-post-pandemic-chronic-absenteeism-into-2025/) in 2021–22 and was still 22.6% in 2024–25 | Deepened the decline; did not start it |
| A changing mix of test-takers | Norway's decline appears within families; adult declines appear at every education level | Weak as the main cause |

The common thread is time and attention. Reading, attendance and practice build verbal and quantitative skill, and each has eroded since the early 2010s. That is Sagan's media worry, delivered by a medium he never saw.

The next test has already begun. In a [field experiment with about 1,000 high-school math students](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), an unrestricted chatbot raised practice scores and then lowered exam scores once it was taken away. A tutor built to make students think avoided the loss.

![An unrestricted chatbot lifted practice scores 48%, then cut exam scores 17%](sagan-ai-chatbot-vs-tutor.png)

How AI enters classrooms may matter more than whether it does.

## 8. Did Sagan overstate it?

Partly. Sagan's own standard requires weighing the optimistic story, and it is real.

- **Americans are far more educated.** The share of adults 25 and older with a bachelor's degree rose from [23.0% in 1995](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/publications/1996/demo/p20-489.pdf) to [about 39% in 2024](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/educational-attainment-data.html).
- **Scientific literacy rose for decades after he wrote.** The share of adults able to follow a newspaper science section [rose from 10% in 1988 to 28% in 2008](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/02/110216110857.htm).
- **The top is holding, and the youngest are recovering.** Strong eighth-graders scored about the same in 2024 as in 2013, U.S. PISA science rose from 489 in 2006 to 502, and 9-year-olds [regained 3 to 4 NAEP points](https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/ltt/2025/) in 2025.
- **Every generation thinks the next is slipping.** In five preregistered studies, adults [judged young people most harshly](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6795513/) on the traits they themselves excel at. Richard Hofstadter won a Pulitzer for [*Anti-intellectualism in American Life*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-intellectualism_in_American_Life) in 1964.
- **The measurements are noisy.** The adult survey changed format, the 2025 U.S. PISA sample has a questionnaire gap, and the main U.S. IQ evidence comes from a self-selected online sample.

Still, the optimistic case is mostly about the years before 2012, and about the top. Credentials kept rising after that; for most Americans, the measures of skill did not.

## 9. Is America getting dumber, or splitting in two?

Splitting. For seventeen years after Sagan wrote, Americans grew more educated and, by most measures, more capable. Then, around 2012, the line turned, not into a slow, uniform rot but into an hourglass: a skilled top that holds or gains, a swelling base that falls, and a thinning middle. The timing fits the arrival of smartphones and feeds better than cultural decay, though nothing here proves cause.

That shape is what makes it a political problem. A uniform decline would make a country slower; an hourglass makes it easier to manipulate. A republic runs on a broad middle that can read a ballot measure, weigh a claim and notice when it is being sold something. The hourglass concentrates technical fluency and money at the narrow top, where since 2010 they convert freely into political power, and leaves the 28% of adults who read at the most basic level or below to get their facts from feeds tuned for engagement. The danger is not that intelligent people will disappear. It is that competence and power are concentrating together while the capacity to scrutinize them grows scarce.

None of this is destiny. The Flynn effect shows that measured skill follows what daily life demands. The levers with evidence behind them are known: attendance first, high-dosage tutoring for the bottom quarter, phone limits tested as experiments, protected time for long-form reading, classroom AI built as a coach rather than an answer machine, a technology office for Congress again, and full disclosure of the money flowing from tech fortunes into elections.

Sagan wrote *The Demon-Haunted World* as a manual, not an obituary. The candle has not gone out. But its light is reaching fewer people, and those left in the dark are increasingly governed by those who hold the flame.
