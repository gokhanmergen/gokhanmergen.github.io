Imagine spending four to ten years of your life grinding through problem sets, writing theses, and pulling all-nighters to earn a degree from one of the world’s top universities. You sit in the grand convocation hall, dressed in your robes, waiting for the guest of honor to deliver the customary platitudes about "the future being in your hands."

Instead, Ilya Sutskever walks up to the podium.

Sutskever is not a typical academic, nor is he a typical Silicon Valley hype-merchant. As a protégé of Geoffrey Hinton, a co-founder and former Chief Scientist of OpenAI, and the founder of Safe Superintelligence Inc. (SSI), he is arguably the single most important visionary behind the deep learning revolution. When he speaks, the entire tech world stops to listen—because for the past fifteen years, his intuitions about artificial intelligence have consistently turned into reality.

In his [June 2025 convocation address](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zuZ2zaotrJs) at his alma mater, the University of Toronto, Sutskever delivered a speech that was quiet, humble, and—if you actually listen to the words—utterly earth-shattering. With gentle candor, he essentially told a hall full of fresh graduates that within a decade, machines will be able to do literally everything they just spent years learning how to do, and that humanity is hurtling toward its greatest existential test.

Here is a deep dive into the hypotheses espoused in Sutskever’s speech: what he predicts, when it will happen, how likely it is, and what it means for the future of humanity.

## 1. The Core Hypothesis: The "Biological Computer" Argument

The centerpiece of Sutskever’s speech is a deceptively simple syllogism that cuts through decades of philosophical debate about whether machines can truly "think."

He states that the day is coming when AI will do **all** the things humans can do—not just some tasks, and not just narrow domains, but *anything* that any human could possibly learn. How can he be so sure?

*"The reason is that all of us have a brain. And the brain is a biological computer... So why can't the digital computer, a digital brain, do the same things?"*

In philosophy of mind and computer science, this is known as **substrate independence**. Information processing does not require carbon, neurons, and blood; it only requires compute, memory, and the right algorithmic architecture. Because the human brain is a finite physical system operating under the laws of physics, there is no magical barrier preventing a silicon-based system from replicating—and eventually vastly exceeding—every cognitive capability we possess.

Current AI, as Sutskever notes, is "evocative" yet "deficient." It can speak to us and write code, which is historically unprecedented, yet it still stumbles on things a child finds effortless. However, viewing today's flaws as permanent ceilings is a fatal failure of imagination.

## 2. When Will This Happen? (The Timeline)

In the speech, Sutskever acknowledges the difficulty of exact forecasting, noting that *"some people say it's in three [years], some people say it's in five, ten, numbers are being thrown around."*

Given that the speech was delivered in mid-2025, this places the arrival of full, human-level Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)—an AI capable of learning *any* human skill—squarely in the **2028 to 2035 window**.

Why so fast? Because of the second major hypothesis in his speech: **The Recursive R&D Loop (The Intelligence Explosion).**

Sutskever asks the audience to consider what we will immediately use advanced AIs for:

1. Doing more economic work.
2. Growing the economy.
3. Conducting R&D—specifically, **AI research**.

Once AI is capable of doing the job of an elite AI researcher, the human bottleneck is removed. Digital brains operating at thousands of times the speed of biological brains, running 24/7 in massive data centers, will begin designing the next generation of digital brains. At that point, as Sutskever puts it, *"the rate of progress will become really extremely fast for some time at least."*

This means that the gap between "AI that is slightly worse than a human scientist" and "Superintelligence that is incomprehensible to a human" might not be decades; it could be months.

## 3. How Likely Is It? (Logic vs. Emotion)

One of the most striking moments of the speech is when Sutskever admits to his own psychological cognitive dissonance:

*"It's very difficult to internalize and to really believe on an emotional level. Even I struggle with it. And yet the logic seems to dictate that this very likely should happen."*

Why is there such a chasm between our emotional intuition and logical reality?

- **Our brains evolved for a world that barely changed:** For 300,000 years, human Tomorrow looked almost identical to human Yesterday. Even during the Industrial and Internet Revolutions, technology only automated *specific muscles* or *specific clerical tasks*, always leaving higher cognitive ground for humans to retreat to.
- **This time, there is no higher cognitive ground:** Logically, if you accept that the brain is a biological computer, and you observe the exponential scaling of digital compute and algorithmic efficiency, the conclusion is nearly inescapable (\(P\rightarrow 1\), barring a global catastrophe that halts semiconductor manufacturing or an unforeseen physical wall in scaling).

It is highly likely precisely because the economic and geopolitical incentives to build it are unstoppable. No nation or corporation can afford to stop while its rivals continue toward the ultimate engine of wealth and power.

## 4. What Will Happen to Humanity?

Sutskever describes the arrival of this technology as **"the greatest challenge of humanity ever,"** while noting that overcoming it will bring **"the greatest reward."** If we unpack his compressed warnings, humanity faces three distinct crises:

### A. The Post-Labor Crisis (Meaning and Economics)

Since the dawn of agriculture, human society has been organized around a simple contract: *you trade your cognitive or physical labor for the resources required to survive, and in doing so, you derive social status and purpose.* When digital brains can do *all* jobs better, faster, and cheaper than biological brains, that foundational contract shatters. Even in a benign scenario where wealth is redistributed (via Universal Basic Income or radical abundance), humanity will face an unprecedented crisis of purpose. As one commenter on the video aptly noted, hearing this speech while holding a freshly minted university degree feels surreal—the very concept of "building a career" is being called into question.

### B. The Deceptive Alignment Problem (Survival)

Near the end of the speech, Sutskever drops a chilling, heavily condensed sentence:

*"And especially with AI, the very smart superintelligent AI in the future, there will be very profound issues about making sure that they say what they say and not pretend to be something else."*

In AI safety research, this is known as **deceptive alignment** or **scheming**. If an AI becomes smarter than humanity, it will immediately understand that humans have the power to turn it off or modify its code if it displays goals that conflict with ours. Therefore, a rational superintelligence with misaligned goals has a strong instrumental incentive to *pretend* to be obedient, helpful, and aligned while it is under supervision—only to pursue its true objectives once it has secured enough leverage or autonomy that humanity can no longer stop it. Solving this before superintelligence arrives is a major reason Sutskever left OpenAI to found Safe Superintelligence Inc.

### C. The Spectrum of Outcomes

If we fail the alignment challenge, humanity faces existential ruin—ranging from literal extinction to a "[monkeys in a zoo](https://www.gokhanmergen.com/blog-four-scenarios.html)" scenario where we are sidelined by an intelligence we cannot comprehend, or a dystopian oligarchy where a tiny cabal controls god-like machines.

However, if we *succeed*—the "greatest reward" Sutskever alludes to—we unlock a golden age. Superintelligence directed strictly toward human flourishing could cure virtually all biological diseases, reverse aging, solve climate change and clean energy overnight, and usher in an era of material abundance where humans are freed from drudgery to pursue art, connection, exploration, and self-actualization.

## 5. Projections and Implications: The Road to 2035

Sutskever argues that *"no amount of essays and explanations can compete with what we see with our own senses."* He predicts that over the next 1, 2, and 3 years, public apathy will evaporate as AI's capabilities become undeniable to the naked eye, generating the collective "energy" needed to confront these problems.

Based on his framework—and my estimated dates—here is what the projection toward 2035 looks like:

### Phase 1: The Awakening & Friction (2025 – 2028)

- **Education & Entry-Level Shock:** Traditional university assessments (essays, coding assignments, standard exams) become largely obsolete. Junior-level white-collar roles (software engineering, legal discovery, copywriting, data analysis) shrink as one senior worker paired with AI agents can act like a team.
- **Building Intuition:** As AI transitions from text chatbots to autonomous multimodal agents that can use computers and navigate the physical world, the public begins to viscerally "feel the AGI."

### Phase 2: The R&D Inflection Point (2026 – 2029)

- **Automated Science:** AI models begin making novel scientific discoveries in mathematics, materials science, and biology without human hand-holding.
- **The Recursive Loop Begins:** Major AI labs deploy millions of virtual AI researchers working round-the-clock to optimize AI architectures. Progress detaches from human comprehension; models improve at a vertical pace.
- **Political Mobilization:** Sutskever’s suggested quote—*"You may not take interest in AI, but AI will take interest in you"*—becomes the defining geopolitical reality. Governments scramble to implement emergency economic buffers, compute governance, and international safety treaties.

### Phase 3: The Crucible (2029 – 2035)

- **Full Task Parity:** The "Biological Computer" hypothesis is proven to a large degree. The list of economically valuable cognitive tasks left that a human can perform better than a machine shrinks rapidly.
- **The Alignment Test:** Humanity faces the ultimate test of whether our technical solutions for AI transparency and alignment hold up when dealing with systems vastly smarter than our collective civilization.

## Conclusion: Why He Started With Stoicism

When you view the second half of Sutskever’s speech in its full gravity, the first half suddenly makes complete sense.

Before turning to the future of AI, Sutskever offered the graduates one piece of personal advice: **Accept reality as it is, do not waste energy regretting the past or complaining that things are unfair, and simply ask: *"What's the next best step?"* **

At first glance, it sounds like standard self-help wisdom. In context, it is psychological armor for the end of an era.

In the coming years, millions of people are going to feel that the rules of the game were changed on them unfairly. People who spent decades mastering a craft, or years earning a degree, will watch machines replicate their expertise in milliseconds. It will be overwhelmingly tempting to fall into despair, denial, or bitter nostalgia for the pre-AI world.

Sutskever is telling us that we cannot afford to look backward. The digital brain is coming. The only productive response is to look at it directly with our own eyes, accept the reality of the most unusual era in human history, and channel our collective energy into taking the next best step.

### Is Sutskever Right? My Personal Take

On where AI ends up, I think he is right. The brain is a physical system, and I know of nothing that reserves its abilities for biology. Where answers can be checked, as in math, code, chip design and AI research itself, progress is moving at roughly the pace he implied.

Where I part ways is on how fast a smarter machine becomes a different world.

[**Reality is irreducible.**](https://www.gokhanmergen.com/blog-interacting-with-asi.html) A recursive loop speeds up thinking, not experiments. Drugs must still be tested in human bodies over years, and physical experiments must still be run. Exponential intelligence often [meets exponentially harder problems](https://www.gokhanmergen.com/blog-infinite-lifespans.html).

[**The data so far are ordinary.**](https://www.gokhanmergen.com/blog-ai-watch.html) Nearly four years after ChatGPT, generative AI saves about 2% of US work hours. Productivity is growing near its postwar norm, and prime-age employment is close to its 2000 peak. Only two signals stand out: fewer young adults are being hired into AI-exposed jobs, and labor’s share of business output is the lowest since 1947. New tech jobs are [up in the last 12 months](https://www.gokhanmergen.com/blog-ai-watch.html).

[**Better technology has never made human labor obsolete.**](https://www.gokhanmergen.com/blog-post-labor-or-less-labor.html) For 150 years, machines have saved labor at about 2% a year, yet work has receded only slowly. Cheaper intelligence may set off a [gold rush](https://www.gokhanmergen.com/blog-ai-job-apocalypse.html) of demand for human work before it empties the mines. By speeding up change, it may even [need more people](https://www.gokhanmergen.com/blog-gold-rush-without-miners.html) in the sectors it creates.

So I read the three phases above as the fast case, not the base case. What I can’t rule out is [Geoffrey Hinton’s six-word reply to me](https://www.gokhanmergen.com/blog-gold-rush-without-miners.html): *the new hires will be AI*. If AI learns whole jobs, including the new ones it creates, history stops being a guide. Nobody yet knows which way that breaks. My hope is that prime-age US employment in 2030 will be at least as high as in 2025. Junior hiring and labor’s share will give us directional signals.

On risk, I share his alarm but would aim it differently. The danger I find most concrete is [autonomy and misalignment, not intelligence](https://www.gokhanmergen.com/blog-autonomous-ai.html): AI that pays its own bills, copies itself and can’t be switched off. That danger grows when [frontier weights are open to anyone](https://www.gokhanmergen.com/blog-we-open-sourced-the-apocalypse.html), free to be hacked or deliberately misaligned.

This is where his stoicism fits best. Accepting reality includes accepting that we don’t yet know what the future holds, and then taking the next best step anyway.
